# Content calendar — organic push before ad season

Why this exists: sporadic blog posts aren't enough on their own. This is a
fixed-cadence, keyword-targeted plan for the runway between now (Oct 2026) and
when the ad campaign starts in spring. Bi-weekly, 6 posts over ~3 months.
Revisit and extend it once these are shipped rather than letting it go stale.

Three pillars, each pointing traffic at a specific money page:

- **A — Landscaping quoting** → interlinks to `/construction-quoting-software`
  and `/construction-estimating-software`. The core business, keep it fed.
- **B — Garden rooms** → same two pages, Garden Rooms angle. **Highest
  priority**: garden-room-specific *pricing* software is nearly uncontested —
  the sites ranking for "garden room cost" and "garden room estimating" are
  either homeowner cost-guides or 3D design/visualizer tools, not quoting
  software. This is the clearest opening we have.
- **C — Trade business / profitability** → broader funnel, same pattern as
  the existing "hidden cost of X" posts (callback, merchant trips, guesstimating)
  that already work well in the brand voice.

Each post should link to at least one other post on this list and to the
relevant software page — don't let them sit as orphans.

## The six posts

### 1. Garden Room Cost UK 2026: What Builders Actually Quote (Pillar B) — ✅ shipped 2026-10-14
- **Target query**: "garden room cost uk" / "how much does a garden room cost"
- **Why**: huge homeowner search volume, currently owned by generic cost-guide
  sites (MyJobQuote, Checkatrade, etc.) with no software angle at all.
- **Angle**: write it from the builder's side, not the homeowner's — real cost
  breakdown logic (frame, cladding, groundwork, glazing) that a builder would
  actually quote from, not a vague £15k–£50k range. Ends with "if you quote
  garden rooms, here's how PriceM8 builds this exact breakdown automatically."
- **Links to**: `/construction-estimating-software`, Pricing page.

### 2. Garden Room Estimating Software: What's Missing from 3D Design Tools (Pillar B)
- **Target query**: "garden room estimating software" / "garden room design
  software"
- **Why**: direct content-gap fill. Everything ranking here is a visualizer
  (draw your garden room in 3D) — none of them price the job.
- **Angle**: "A pretty render doesn't tell you if the job makes money." Contrast
  design tools vs pricing tools honestly — they're not really competitors, a
  builder might use both — then explain what PriceM8 actually calculates
  (frame, cladding choice, groundwork) that a visualizer never touches.
- **Links to**: `/construction-quoting-software`, post #1.

### 3. How to Quote a Patio Job in the UK: A Real Breakdown (Pillar A)
- **Target query**: "how to quote a patio job" / "patio quote breakdown"
- **Why**: bridges existing MOT/takeoff-heavy brand content to a concrete
  worked example — the kind of specific, numbers-first post that works for
  both human readers and AI-search extraction.
- **Angle**: one real patio size, real MOT tonnage, real waste %, real labour
  day-rate — show the maths PriceM8 does automatically, step by step.
- **Links to**: `/construction-estimating-software`, the existing
  "hidden-cost-of-guesstimating" post.

### 4. Landscaping Quoting Software vs a Spreadsheet: What Actually Changes (Pillar A)
- **Target query**: "landscaping quoting software"
- **Why**: this is the closest direct-match keyword to
  `/construction-quoting-software`'s real positioning, and we don't have a
  single piece of content targeting it head-on yet.
- **Angle**: honest comparison, not a hit piece — most landscapers currently
  quote from a notebook or a spreadsheet, not switching from a competitor.
  Show the specific failure modes (stale material prices, no margin applied
  automatically, no PDF) and how each is solved.
- **Links to**: `/construction-quoting-software`, "why-spreadsheets-are-killing-your-trade-business" (existing post).

### 5. Why Landscapers Undercharge for Groundwork (Pillar C)
- **Target query**: "landscaping pricing mistakes" / "undercharging landscaping"
- **Why**: continues the profitability-advice pattern that's already working
  (turnover-vs-profit, hidden-cost-of-guesstimating), fresh long-tail angle.
- **Angle**: groundwork/sub-base specifically — it's invisible once the job's
  done, so it's the easiest place to shave corners on quote and regret it on
  site. Real MOT/compaction numbers, tie back to takeoff accuracy.
- **Links to**: "hidden-cost-of-guesstimating" (existing), post #3.

### 6. Should You Charge for Quotes? (Pillar C) — upgraded scope 2026-10-22
- **Target query**: "should I charge for quotes UK" / "free quotes landscaping"
  — an active, ongoing debate on trade social media, not just a search term.
- **Why**: same "small thing, bigger cost than you think" format as
  "the-50-pound-nip-to-the-merchant" and "real-cost-of-a-callback", but the
  real angle is sharper than a stat: the debate assumes a quote costs the same
  to produce either way, and that's the part that's actually changed. Good
  AI-search citation bait (concrete numbers) and a genuine take, not just
  restating both sides of the argument.
- **Angle**: take a real position — free quotes make sense when they're cheap
  to produce, expensive when they're not. Time cost of unpaid site visits,
  contrasted with how fast a PriceM8 quote is to produce from a phone.
- **Links to**: "real-cost-of-a-callback" (existing), Pricing page.
- **Multi-channel**: this one isn't just a blog post. Three pieces off the
  same core argument, published together: (1) long-form on the blog, SEO
  angle; (2) a spin on the PriceM8 Instagram/Facebook, more product-forward;
  (3) a personal-voice single-image post on the separate "Michal the
  Landscaper" Instagram — real opinion, PriceM8 barely or not named, the
  account's value is Michal looking credible, not a pitch.

### 7. Comebacks and Snags That Aren't Your Fault (Pillar C)
- **Target query**: long-tail, liability/communication angle, not yet scoped
  against real search volume.
- **Why**: distinct from the existing "real-cost-of-a-callback" post, which
  is about *your*-fault callbacks and avoiding them with checklists. This is
  a liability/communication problem instead — what to put in writing on a
  quote to protect yourself, and how to push back when blamed for something
  outside your control (ground conditions, customer-caused damage, a
  material defect).
- **Status**: queued, not yet scoped in detail. Revisit after #6 ships.

## After these six

Don't let the calendar run dry — once these ship, pull the next six from real
signal: Search Console queries that are getting impressions but low
click-through (title/meta needs work, not new content), and anything from the
directory listings' own on-site search that suggests unmet demand.
